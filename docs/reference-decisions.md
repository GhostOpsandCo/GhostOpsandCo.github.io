## System-level conclusions

1. Premium sites own a recognizable visual world. They do not get there through glass cards or a dark theme alone.
2. The hero needs one signature asset: Elijah plus a real product, or a real product transformed into an art-directed evidence object.
3. The portfolio must behave like a commercial proof system: problem, decision, working artifact, role/boundary, then next action.
4. Real screenshots should be shown cinematically and legibly, not reduced to tiny thumbnails or mixed into fictional dashboards.
5. One accent family should connect the hero, annotations, case-study markers, focus states, and contact flow.
6. Motion should reveal causality or hierarchy. It should not be ambient decoration.
7. Mobile is a distinct composition: promise, CTA, and first proof object must survive at 390px without relying on desktop collage behavior.
8. Contact should be a real, short conversion path. A mailto link alone is not sufficient.

### Active page anatomy

1. Compact navigation and one persistent `Request a fit call` action until a scheduler is verified.
2. A first-viewport answer to five questions: what Elijah does, who should hire him, what proof shows skill, who the human is, and what to do next.
3. Elijah's exact headshot as an authorship signal, paired with real product proof rather than replacing it.
4. The latest Orelis site artifact, Team Take Down creator workspace, exact Treasury Router advisor dashboard, actual GridSynapse dashboard, and Monarch Shield site artifact as inspectable product evidence.
5. A compact proof index that lets buyers jump to the product closest to their problem before reading the full project run.
6. A truthful systems sequence: six inspectable systems and their exact states, with no borrowed customer logos.
7. A three-step mechanism: find the bottleneck, design the operating logic, build the testable product.
8. Every project section must show summary, use case, who it is for, proof, benefits, role, current evidence, and boundary.
9. A real fit-call or short-brief conversion surface that does not claim provider delivery until verified.

### Direction-specific rejection tests

- Reject if the page could be mistaken for a generic AI consultant, magazine, resume, personal blog, or SaaS company with invented customer proof.
- Reject if removing the background color makes the design indistinguishable from a generic centered SaaS hero plus dashboard screenshot.
- Reject if the grid stops after the hero or is used only as decoration.
- Reject if the product theater uses reconstructed, invented, or tiny unreadable product UI.
- Reject if Treasury Router is described as live client-fund execution or autonomous execution.
- Reject if Team Take Down claims whole-internet coverage, guaranteed discovery, or guaranteed removals.
- Reject if Monarch Shield claims runtime enforcement, fraud prevention, wallet security, regulatory compliance, or settlement validation.
- Reject if Elijah's exact PFP and concise two-sentence personal introduction are absent from the hero.
- Reject if the layout becomes a repeated bento-card gallery.
- Reject if the active references in `docs/reference-selection-matrix.md` cannot each be mapped to a specific rendered element.
- Reject if any visual effect outranks the real product evidence or the primary conversion action.

## Explicitly rejected

- The first generated concept set from this redesign is rejected and must not be implemented. It was accepted after too little research and produced generic open columns, flat monochrome surfaces, no signature object, and misassigned product imagery.
- Editorial Intelligence V2.1 is rejected and must not be implemented. It overcorrected into a warm authored portfolio, made the portrait the signature object, and abandoned the requested high-end AI/SaaS product language and Profound-led structure.
- Decorative glassmorphism, dark AI gradients, repeated bento grids, fake dashboards, unsupported social proof, equal-weight CTAs, paid typography, and animation without a comprehension or state purpose.
- A centered headline above a dashboard screenshot as the dominant template. Peec AI executes that pattern well, but it would make Elijah look like another SaaS product rather than the product builder a buyer is hiring.
- A dense career archive on the homepage. Depth belongs in case studies and the resume; the homepage must create comprehension and qualified action quickly.

## Direction gate

V8.5 is the active implementation direction: a palette-preserving editorial homepage that keeps the current site language, keeps each major product in a dedicated copy-first section, and tightens the page into a single top-down reading path with a compact proof index before the project run. Profound informs the singular hero spine, product-story discipline, module-path clarity, and black/white section rhythm; Hyros informs direct offer rows, use-case clarity, proof placement, and specific actions. Neither source changes the palette or permits copied language, copied layouts, borrowed proof, invented results, mint/teal chapter washes, scroll hijacking, or decorative motion. Production release still requires explicit approval after responsive QA and independent review.
